# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Founders and owners of 7 to 8 figure Shopify brands. They wear several hats,
they are the person who decides which apps go on the store, and they are
reading this between other jobs. They already know what a post purchase
support ticket costs them; they do not need the problem explained. What they
are weighing is whether this is trustworthy enough to put on live orders and
quick enough to be worth the afternoon.

Their context: desktop, mid scroll, comparing two or three apps in adjacent
tabs.

## Product Purpose

Sell ClickPost Order Editing & Upsells to Shopify merchants. Shoppers fix
their own orders after checkout (address, contact details, items, discount
codes, cancellation) and see a one tap upsell while they are there.

Success is a single action: **install from the Shopify App Store**. The
booking form at the end of the page is a fallback for larger stores, not the
target. Every section is either evidence that the app is safe to install or a
step toward the Install button.

## Brand Personality

Plain spoken, evidence led, precise. The page shows real product screens, real
merchant reviews and real numbers, and lets those carry the argument. No
adjective appears that the evidence has not already earned.

Note: derived from the page as it stands, not yet stated by the owner. Worth
confirming before it drives a large change.

## Anti-references

**Generic SaaS template.** Specifically: purple or indigo gradient hero,
blurred colour blobs behind the fold, three identical icon plus heading plus
paragraph cards, a testimonial carousel nobody reads, stock abstract shapes
standing in for product. If a section could be lifted onto any other B2B site
by swapping the noun, it has failed.

## Design Principles

1. **Content is fixed, design is the variable.** The owner wrote the copy and
   chose what is on the page. Improve how it looks, reads and paces. Do not
   cut sections, shorten copy or drop content to make a layout easier.
2. **Evidence over adjectives.** A real screenshot, a merchant's own words or
   a figure that reconciles beats any claim about the product.
3. **Real screens, never illustrations.** Product mocks are built from the
   actual interface. When a number cannot be sourced, say so rather than
   drawing it.
4. **Motion has to earn its keep.** The page has a lot of it: a rotating
   globe, a 3D case study ring, scroll driven steps. Each has to be doing a
   job, and each has to stop for anyone who asks it to.
5. **Everything points at Install.** Sections may inform or reassure, but none
   should become a destination of its own.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA.

- 4.5:1 on body text, 3:1 on large text and meaningful non text.
- Everything interactive is keyboard reachable with a visible focus state.
  This matters more than usual here: the language list and the globe are
  buttons that no longer look like buttons.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured by the globe, the case study ring, the
  step transitions and the FAQ. Anything added later joins that list.
- Video is click to play, never autoplay with sound.
- Colour is never the only carrier of meaning.
